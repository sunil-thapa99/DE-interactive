Technologies

Snowflake + AWS

What it is: A cloud-native data warehouse (compute and storage scaled independently) commonly paired with AWS S3 as a raw data source for ingestion pipelines.

Why use it: Near-zero-maintenance scaling, pay-per-second compute, native support for semi-structured data, and built-in features (Snowpipe, Streams, Dynamic Tables, secure data sharing) that replace a lot of infrastructure you'd otherwise have to build yourself.

Covers: S3 ingestion via Snowpipe, three ETL orchestration patterns (Tasks+Streams, Dynamic Tables, dbt+Airflow), architecture & performance, governance, data modeling, and interview Q&A.

Available now 📡

Apache Kafka

What it is: A distributed event streaming platform for publishing, storing, and processing continuous streams of records in real time.

Why use it: Decouples producers from consumers at high throughput/low latency, and is the de facto backbone for real-time pipelines and event-driven architectures across most modern data stacks.

Covers: The distributed log, topics/partitions/offsets, producer durability & ordering knobs, consumer groups & rebalancing, replication & ISR, exactly-once & transactions, Connect/Schema Registry/Streams, production ops & tuning, and interview Q&A.

Available now 🔥

Apache Spark + PySpark + Databricks

What it is: A distributed compute engine for processing large datasets across a cluster, programmed via the DataFrame API (PySpark/Scala/SQL), most commonly run on the Databricks platform with Delta Lake as its storage layer.

Why use it: Handles data volumes and transformation logic too large or too custom for pure SQL — arbitrary code transforms, ML feature engineering, and a single unified engine spanning batch and streaming, unlike a single-node tool like pandas.

Covers: A hands-on Databricks Community Edition pipeline, Python/PySpark fundamentals, the DataFrame API, architecture & performance tuning (shuffles, caching, AQE, skew), Structured Streaming, Delta Lake, Spark-vs-Snowflake trade-offs, and interview Q&A.

Available now 🏃

Apache Airflow

What it is: A workflow orchestration platform for scheduling and monitoring pipelines defined as code (DAGs).

Why use it: The standard choice when a pipeline needs to coordinate multiple systems (not just one warehouse), with retries, dependency management, and observability built in.

Covers: DAG authoring (TaskFlow, dynamic mapping), scheduling & the execution_date gotcha, backfills & idempotency, sensors vs. deferrable operators, XComs, executors & concurrency, and interview Q&A with follow-ups.

Available now

Databases

The two data stores a DE is expected to reason about — the relational/SQL world and the document/NoSQL world — each with when-to-use judgment, modeling, query & performance tuning, and interview Q&A.

📊

SQL & Python Coding

What it is: The relational/SQL side — the live-coding round where you solve SQL (and PySpark/Python) problems at the keyboard while thinking aloud.

Why practice it: Almost every DE loop has a coding screen; the difference between mid and senior is naming the tradeoff and the failure mode, not just producing a passing query.

Covers: Window functions, dedup, gaps-and-islands, top-N per group, query optimization; PySpark transforms, joins/broadcast, skew/salting, UDF-vs-native; plus live-coding Q&A.

Available now 🍃

MongoDB & NoSQL

What it is: The document/NoSQL side — when to choose MongoDB over a relational DB, how to model documents, and how it scales.

Why practice it: The trap interviewers set is treating Mongo like a relational DB; senior answers turn on embedding-vs-referencing, shard-key choice, and knowing when NOT to use it.

Covers: NoSQL taxonomy & when-to-use, document modeling patterns & anti-patterns, the aggregation pipeline & $lookup, indexing (ESR rule, explain), replica sets & sharding, and interview Q&A.

Available now

Interview practice

Cross-cutting rounds that aren't tied to one technology — structured basic→advanced, every question hides its model answer until you expand (form your own answer first), and each ends with the follow-up cross-questions a real interviewer digs with.

🏗️

DE System Design

What it is: The open-ended "design a pipeline for X" round where you drive the whiteboard.

Why practice it: Senior/staff loops hinge on this — it tests whether you can clarify requirements, choose batch vs. streaming, and reason about scale, failure, and cost out loud.

Covers: A repeatable framework, building blocks (lakehouse/medallion, CDC, idempotency, data contracts), and full case studies (fraud/risk, HL7/FHIR claims, CDC sync, DWH modeling) with scaling & cost follow-ups.

Available now 💬

Behavioral & Resume Deep-Dive

What it is: STAR-format behavioral questions plus the technical cross-questions interviewers ask about your actual resume bullets.

Why practice it: The hiring-manager round is won or lost on specific, quantified stories — and every impressive bullet invites a "how exactly did you do that?" you need a crisp answer for.

Covers: The STAR method, project deep-dives (the batch→Airflow migration, HIPAA/PHI, on-call RCA, cost right-sizing), leadership & conflict, and classic questions with a ready 60-second pitch.
